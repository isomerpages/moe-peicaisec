<p>On 24 July 2026, three Secondary 2 students from class 2-Care, Starlett
Seng, Angel Ong, and Javan Pek, had the honour of representing Peicai at
the SHECARES Open House, held at the SHECARES Centre run by SG Her Empowerment
(SHE). The event was graced by the Guest-of-Honour, Minister for Law and
Second Minister for Home Affairs, Mr Edwin Tong, who officially marked
the occasion with remarks on the importance of online safety and the role
of community in supporting survivors of online harm.</p>
<p>The students’ participation was the culmination of their class Values-in-Action
(VIA) project on online harms, undertaken as part of Peicai’s “Learn, Act,
Pass-It-On” VIA framework. As Secondary 2 students, they were at the “Act”
stage of their VIA journey, translating their understanding of a social
cause into meaningful, community-facing action. Class 2-Care had prepared
a range of artefacts specially for the open house, which drew members of
the public across both 24 and 25 July 2026.</p>
<p>The students set up three interactive stations at the event. The first
featured a publicity video produced by the class, introducing visitors
to what online harms look like and how youths can seek help. The second
station displayed a series of infographics that communicated key findings
on youth perceptions of online harms, helping visitors understand the scale
and impact of the issue among young people. The third station was a pledge
board corner, where members of the public were invited to write a short
personal note committing to take action against online harms, a simple
but powerful way to engage the community in collective responsibility.</p>
<p>A highlight of the day was the opportunity for Starlett, Angel, and Javan
to personally walk Minister Edwin Tong through their interactive display.
Starlett was subsequently featured in The Straits Times in a photograph
showing her introducing the Minister to the students’ work, a proud moment
for the school. The students also had the privilege of meeting Mr Francis
Ng, Singapore’s first Commissioner of Online Safety, as well as Peicai
Secondary School’s School Advisory Committee member, Ms Evangeline Tan.</p>
<p>SHE, the organising non-profit behind SHECARES, recognised the students’
contributions in their event publicity, noting that the Peicai students
brought a valuable youth perspective to the open house through their original
videos, student-created works, and the public pledge board.</p>
<p>For Starlett, Angel, and Javan, the open house was far more than a showcase.
It was an opportunity to witness their class VIA project come to life and
see firsthand the impact their work had on real visitors and guests. Stepping
out of their comfort zones to interact with distinguished guests, including
a Cabinet Minister and Singapore's first Commissioner of Online Safety,
the students demonstrated the Peicai values of Responsibility and Care,
recognising their duty to their community and acting with compassion to
contribute to a safer online environment for all. Their resilience shone
through as they navigated the demands of a large-scale public event with
courage and adaptability, engaging confidently with members of the public
and dignitaries alike.</p>
<p>The experience also deepened the students' socio-emotional competencies.
Through meaningful interactions with guests and the public, they strengthened
their communication and collaboration skills, exercised responsible decision-making,
and developed greater social awareness of the challenges faced by online
harm survivors. Engaging with a cause that extends beyond Singapore's shores
also broadened their civic and global literacy, helping them appreciate
the wider significance of the work they had contributed to. Most importantly,
the students came away with a deep sense of pride, knowing that the effort
they had poured into their VIA project was worthwhile and had made a genuine
difference.</p>
<p>The students’ involvement in this meaningful event reflects the spirit
of Peicai's VIA programme, which seeks to nurture compassionate leaders
who serve their communities with purpose. By advocating for online safety
and engaging the public in conversations about digital responsibility,
2-Care students, represented by Starlett, Angel, and Javan demonstrated
exactly what it means to lead by serving others. Peicai Secondary School
is immensely proud of their dedication, confidence, and commitment to making
a difference.</p>
