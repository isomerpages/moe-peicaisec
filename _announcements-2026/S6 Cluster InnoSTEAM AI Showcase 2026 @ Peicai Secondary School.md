<p>On <strong>26 August</strong>, Peicai Secondary School hosted the <strong>S6 Cluster InnoSTEAM AI Showcase 2026</strong>,
bringing together students from <strong>Peicai Secondary School, Bartley Secondary School, St. Gabriel’s Secondary School and Zhonghua Secondary School</strong> for
an afternoon of sharing, exploration and conversations about Artificial
Intelligence (AI).</p>
<p>Centred on the question, <strong>“How can we harness AI to enhance learning without outsourcing our thinking?”</strong>,
the showcase encouraged students to go beyond discovering what AI can do
and consider <strong>when and how it should be used purposefully, responsibly and discerningly</strong>.
Students were challenged to explore the possibilities of AI while questioning
its outputs, recognising its limitations and considering what thinking
and decisions should remain their own.</p>
<p>A highlight of the afternoon was the sharing by our <strong>12 Secondary 1 and 2 Class Tech Champions</strong>,
who presented six projects exploring different ways in which AI and technology
could support their learning and CCA experiences. These ranged from student-developed
solutions such as a <strong>CCA Progress Tracker</strong> and <strong>Homework Calendar</strong>,
to explorations of AI-enabled tools and approaches for enhancing learning.</p>
<p>Through these student-led sharings, participants were invited not merely
to admire what the technology could do, but to think critically about each
application: <strong>Did AI genuinely enhance learning? What important thinking still needed to be done by the learner? What should we check or question before trusting an AI-generated response?</strong> Considerations
such as accuracy, bias, sources, privacy and over-reliance formed part
of these conversations.</p>
<p>Students also heard from our <strong>keynote speaker, Ms Khor Le Yi, Co-founder of Ottodot</strong>,
who shared how AI is being used in her work, giving students a glimpse
into its <strong>real-world applications, possibilities and limitations</strong>.
Her sharing also prompted students to reflect on their own use of AI and,
importantly, how <strong>human judgment, creativity and critical thinking remain essential even as AI becomes increasingly capable</strong>.</p>
<p>The afternoon concluded with a <strong>cross-school round-table discussion</strong>,
where students exchanged perspectives with peers from the four schools.
They discussed where AI could add value, what should remain human thinking,
and even areas where they held different views — creating opportunities
to listen to, question and learn from one another.</p>
<p>More than a showcase of AI projects, the <strong>S6 Cluster InnoSTEAM AI Showcase</strong> was
an opportunity for students to grow as thoughtful and discerning users
of technology. As AI continues to evolve, we hope our students will not
only ask <strong>“What can AI do for me?”</strong>, but also <strong>“How can I use AI to deepen my learning while keeping the thinking, judgment and responsibility mine?”</strong>
</p>
